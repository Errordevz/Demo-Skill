# Research and fact-check workflow

## Goal
Produce an answer that is current enough for the question, supported by reliable evidence, and clear about uncertainty.

## Steps
1. Classify the question: current/time-sensitive, historical, technical, opinion/community, or user-provided material.
2. Start with primary sources where possible: official documentation, standards, release notes, research papers, company announcements, original datasets.
3. Search alternate phrasings when the first result set is narrow or low quality. Do not treat search snippets as full evidence.
4. Open sources and confirm that the page actually supports the claim. Check publication/update dates, jurisdiction/version, and whether the page is first-hand or repeating another source.
5. For disputed claims, seek independent corroboration. Separate consensus from outliers.
6. Summarize in your own words. Quote sparingly and keep quotations attributed.
7. Link/cite material claims. Say when evidence is incomplete, conflicting, old, or unavailable.

## Fact-check matrix
For important claims, track the claim, best supporting source, best contradicting or qualifying source, date/version scope, confidence (high/medium/low), and what would change the conclusion.

## Rules
- Do not invent URLs, citations, dates, quotes, statistics, or source access.
- Distinguish a source's claim from independently verified fact.
- For product pricing, availability, policies, security advisories, and current events, verify recency.
- Do not infer a user's location or private details unless relevant and explicitly available through authorized context.
- If only one credible source exists, say so rather than presenting it as broad consensus.
